## 0. Why this exists

A hard pivot back into Static Live exposed a larger possibility:

> The Collective may already contain most of the organs required for a provenance-preserving live media organism whose native input is human occurrence and whose outputs can be live, episodic, musical, visual, textual, archival, or composed from several of those at once.

The key move is not "put AI in the livestream."

The key move is:

```text
OCCURRENCE
  ↓
PRESERVE
  ↓
BREATHE THROUGH MANY NATIVE REPRESENTATIONS
  ↓
ADMIT ONE OR MORE CONSEQUENCES
  ↓
EXPRESS
  ↓
RECEIPT
  ↓
NEW OCCURRENCE
```

This target preserves the current idea before implementation pressure flattens it into one app, one model, one transcript, or one pipeline.

# 2. The central target

## THE COLLECTIVE AS A LIVE TRANSDUCER

Working statement:

> **One occurrence propagates through a body whose organs each express it in their own native representational language, while the source occurrence and every transformation path remain attributable.**

Not:

```text

→ transcript
→ transcript becomes reality
→ everything else
```

Instead:

```text
LIVE OCCURRENCE
      │
      ├── exact audio carrier
      ├── time ranges
      ├── declared source/channel
      ├── speaker candidates
      ├── rhythm / prosody
      ├── silence
      ├── music / room sound
      └── transcript candidate
              │
              ▼
           APERTURE
              │
              ▼
        TRANSFORM BODY
```

Founding boundary:

```text
TRANSCRIPT != AUDIO
```

The transcript is one projection of the occurrence.

It may be corrected without rewriting the sound.

The sound may carry meaningful distinctions not present in the transcript.

# 6. Three temporal fields

A single live or recorded session may contain:

```text
PAST
Autodiscography / Vault / lineage / receipts

PRESENT
room audio / people / performance / environment

POSSIBLE
transforms / memory influence / mutations /
visuals / relations / edits / scene candidates
```

Iron Lung receives:

```text
PAST + PRESENT
       ↓
many POSSIBLES
       ↓
LUNG
```

Most possibilities never become consequences.

An admitted candidate becomes:

```text
possible
  ↓
ADMIT
  ↓
HEART
  ↓
new PRESENT
  ↓
preserved
  ↓
new PAST
```

Operational recursion:

```text
PAST[n] + PRESENT[n]
        ↓
    POSSIBILITY[n]
        ↓
  admitted expression
        ↓
    PRESENT[n+1]
        ↓
      preserved
        ↓
      PAST[n+1]
```

# 17. What appears novel here

Do not claim novelty merely because the composition feels unusual.

Many external systems already solve:

- realtime media transport;
- podcast publishing;
- STT;
- transcript editing;
- media graphs;
- synchronization;
- video switching;
- render/export;
- agent participation.

The candidate novelty is narrower:

> **A provenance-preserving media metabolism where exact occurrence, interpretation, historical memory, generated possibility, editorial admission, physical consequence, and return-to-memory remain separately attributable across live, podcast, radio, music, visual, textual, and archival expression.**

In particular:

```text
what was present
what was derived
what became possible
what history influenced it
what was admitted
what entered the world afterward
```

remain independently inspectable.

This claim remains a target hypothesis until executable specimens demonstrate it.

# 18. MADD pressure questions

Before promoting this target, attack it.

1. Is Occurrence actually a useful shared primitive, or an attractive mega-object that will collapse project authority?
2. Can audio remain first-class once transcript-centric interfaces become convenient?
3. Can we use one transform grammar without manufacturing a hidden shared ontology?
4. Can historical retrieval influence expression without becoming implicit prompt authority?
5. Does Heart add a real invariant, or merely rename an existing admission boundary?
6. Can PipeWire / GStreamer carry physical flow while project-native objects preserve semantic authority cleanly?
7. Does OpenTimelineIO preserve enough provenance for our editing law, or do we need a sidecar receipt model?
8. Can a current transcript correction be represented without falsifying the original ASR occurrence?
9. Can podcast editing preserve laughter, silence, overlap, music, and prosody as first-class source material?
10. Can a live transform system remain useful when every AI transform provider is unplugged and replaced by deterministic fixtures?
11. Can two organs disagree without an automatic resolver?
12. Can a rich proposal field terminate with no action and still count as success?
13. Can public Podcasting 2.0 outputs remain projections rather than becoming the archive authority?
14. What is the smallest specimen that would falsify the claim that this is one organism rather than several loosely related tools?
